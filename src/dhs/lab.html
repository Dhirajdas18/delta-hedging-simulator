Delta band: with the band rule, you trade only when the futures you should hold differ from what you hold by more than this many units (0.05 = 5% of one unit of NIFTY).

Sold and hedged: profit or loss at expiry

How to read it: each bar counts the simulated paths whose profit or loss at expiry fell in that range, after hedging and costs. Where the bars sit (above or below zero) comes mostly from the gap between the volatility you sold at and the one that happens; how wide they spread is what hedging couldn’t remove. Hover over a bar for details.

Sold, not hedged (for comparison)

The same option sold with no hedge: most paths keep the whole premium (the tall bar on the right) and a few lose many times more. Note the much wider scale than the chart above.

The formula explains it

How to read it: each dot is one path. Across is what a simple formula predicts the hedged profit should be from that path’s daily moves; up is what the simulated hedge actually made. Dots on the grey diagonal mean the formula was exactly right. A day with a big move costs the seller money and a quiet day earns it, in proportion to how sensitive the hedge was that day. Hover over a dot for the numbers.

Three sample paths

How to read it: the top panel is NIFTY futures over the days to expiry for three simulated paths; the bottom panel is how many units of futures the hedge held at each point. As NIFTY rises the hedge of a sold call holds more futures, and as it falls it holds fewer; near expiry it swings towards all or nothing depending on which side of the strike NIFTY is.
